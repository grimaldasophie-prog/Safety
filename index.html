The problem

Every safety product today assumes she can still act.

Panic buttons and SOS apps need her to notice the danger, reach her phone and press something. When a drink is spiked or something happens fast, that moment never comes. By the time anything feels wrong, she can't call anyone.

Inside the bracelet

Jewelry on the outside. A phone's worth of radio inside.

No screen, no visible button, no logo. The inlay panel is the only touch surface. Everything that matters runs on the bracelet itself, so it keeps working when her phone is gone.

Cellular + GPS

Standalone connection

A system-in-package keeps a live link so an alert can always go out. Bluetooth to the phone is a convenience only.

Touch + LRA motor

30-minute check-in

A short, private pulse. One tap on the inlay confirms. Two minutes without a reply sends her location to her circle.

Accelerometer

Fall detection

A sudden impact followed by stillness triggers an alert on its own, between check-ins.

Clasp sensor

Removal alarm

Opening the clasp at home is safe. Anywhere else, it alerts her circle with "Clasp opened, last location".

Dual confirmation

Two-person shut-off

Ending the night away from home needs her and one person from her circle. One person under pressure can't switch it off.

Backup capacitor

A last signal

If power is cut suddenly, the bracelet still sends a final location. Target battery: 18–24 hours of active use.

Design

Something she'd wear anyway.

A safety device only works if she actually wears it, so Anchor looks like real jewelry and not like a gadget. It's a polished hinged bangle with an inlay panel. She taps the inlay to check in, and the removal sensor sits in the clasp.

Finish
Gold · White

Six launch finishes in three metals, with a choice of inlay. Every variant has the same electronics inside, so offering more finishes adds style options without adding engineering work.

The ask

A staged raise that buys evidence before hardware.

Instead of asking for the full build cost up front, we raise in two steps. Stage 2 only happens if Stage 1 proves real demand and a workable hardware plan.

Risks we plan for

Hardware is slow and costly

The staged raise keeps the first commitment small until a functional prototype proves the battery and signal targets.

Signal in basements and clubs

A core question for the feasibility study. The backup capacitor and last-known location cover short dropouts.

Big tech adds a similar feature

Watches are general gadgets. Anchor is jewelry built for one job, and the two-person shut-off may be patentable.

Wearing it every night out

Our survey tests whether the check-in feels reassuring or annoying, and whether $189 feels fair. The interval adapts to what users tell us.
